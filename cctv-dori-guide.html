What is DORI in CCTV? A Complete Guide

1. What does DORI mean?

A camera may capture a person from far away without showing enough detail to identify them. DORI helps engineers plan the level of detail required at different distances.

LevelTraditional thresholdPurpose
Detection25 px/mDetect presence
Observation62.5 px/mObserve activities
Recognition125 px/mRecognize a familiar person
Identification250 px/mProvide identification-level detail

These are commonly used traditional IEC 62676-4 DORI planning thresholds, not guarantees of actual identification.

Detection

Determine whether someone is present, for example at a perimeter.

Observation

Observe general clothing, movement and activity in a warehouse or corridor.

Recognition

Obtain enough detail to potentially recognize a familiar person at an entrance.

Identification

Obtain detailed images for identity assessment at critical points such as access control doors.

2. How DORI coverage works

As distance increases, a person occupies fewer pixels. The strongest achievable DORI level therefore changes with distance.

3. Factors affecting DORI

Resolution: More horizontal pixels generally increase DORI distance at the same viewing angle.

Focal length: A longer lens narrows the view and increases detail at a distance for the same sensor.

Active sensor width: The effective imaging width in millimeters. Nominal sensor formats are approximate; check the camera datasheet for actual HFOV or active width.

Lighting and image quality: Noise, focus, motion blur, compression and camera positioning affect usable detail.

5. Pixel density (PPM)

Pixels per meter measures horizontal image detail across the scene.

PPM = Horizontal pixels ÷ Scene width (m)

For a 1920-pixel image covering 10 meters, PPM = 1920 ÷ 10 = 192 px/m. This meets Recognition but not Identification.

6. Calculation formulas

HFOV = 2 × atan(S ÷ (2 × f))

S is active sensor width and f is focal length, both in mm. Convert the angle to degrees if required.

Scene width W = 2 × d × tan(HFOV ÷ 2)

d is the target distance in meters. Use consistent angle units.

DORI distance = H ÷ [2 × P × tan(HFOV ÷ 2)]

H is horizontal resolution in pixels and P is the required pixel density. These formulas assume ideal rectilinear projection and a perpendicular target plane.

11. Frequently asked questions

What does DORI stand for?

Detection, Observation, Recognition and Identification.

Is 2.8 mm better than 4 mm?

Neither is universally better. The shorter lens usually covers a wider area for the same sensor.

How many pixels per meter are needed for identification?

The traditional DORI threshold is 250 px/m.

Does H.265 affect DORI?

Not the geometric calculation, but excessive compression may reduce usable detail.

Does frame rate affect DORI?

Not geometric pixel density, but it affects motion capture.

Does DORI apply to analog cameras?

Yes, using effective image resolution and field of view.

Is the calculator free?

Yes. It also supports PDF reports.

Conclusion

Choose cameras based on the required detail at the target location, not megapixels alone. Use DORI for planning and confirm performance under actual site conditions.
